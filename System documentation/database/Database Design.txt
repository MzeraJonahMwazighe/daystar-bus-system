# Daystar University Bus Booking & Fleet Management System

## Database Design Definition

### Version 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose of the Database

The database serves as the central repository for the Daystar University Bus Booking and Fleet Management System. It stores, manages, protects, and organizes all operational data required for transporting students between campuses and supporting university transport operations.

Unlike a traditional booking database, this database is designed to support the complete transport lifecycle, beginning from trip planning by administrators to ticket validation during boarding and reporting after a trip has been completed.

The database must ensure that every booking, payment, seat assignment, bus allocation, and administrative action can be accurately recorded, tracked, audited, and retrieved.

The system is designed to support approximately **500–700 students daily**, with room for future expansion without requiring structural redesign.

---

# 2. Vision of the Database

The vision of the database is to provide a centralized, scalable, secure, and reliable data management system that supports all transport operations at Daystar University.

The database is designed to become the single source of truth for every transport activity performed by students, drivers, administrators, and supervisors.

Rather than storing information only for bookings, the database is intended to manage the university's entire transport ecosystem.

---

The database shall:

# 5. Database Design Philosophy

The database follows several important principles.

---

## 5.1 Normalization

The database will follow relational database normalization principles to eliminate duplicate data.

Information should exist only once wherever possible.

This improves:

* consistency
* maintainability
* reporting
* storage efficiency

---

## 5.2 Scalability

The database must allow future expansion without changing the existing structure.

Future expansion may include:

* additional campuses
* additional buses
* additional administrators
* additional drivers
* additional routes
* mobile application integration
* student authentication
* GPS tracking

---

## 5.3 Security

Sensitive information shall never be duplicated unnecessarily.

Every important action must be traceable.

Only authorized users may perform administrative functions.

---

## 5.4 Data Integrity

The database must ensure:

* bookings reference valid trips
* payments reference valid bookings
* tickets reference successful payments
* buses cannot be double-booked
* seats cannot be sold twice
* deleted records are archived instead of permanently removed whenever appropriate

---

## 5.5 Reliability

The database should support:

* transaction rollback
* backup
* recovery
* logging
* error recovery

No payment should be lost because of partial failures.

      │

# 11. Database Design Principles

The database shall adhere to the following principles:

* Every entity shall have a unique identifier (Primary Key).
* Relationships shall be enforced through Foreign Keys.
* Data redundancy shall be minimized through normalization.
* Transactions shall guarantee consistency.
* Auditability shall be maintained for all critical operations.
* Soft deletion shall be preferred over permanent deletion where historical records are required.
* Sensitive operations shall be role-based.
* The design shall prioritize scalability, maintainability, and security.
